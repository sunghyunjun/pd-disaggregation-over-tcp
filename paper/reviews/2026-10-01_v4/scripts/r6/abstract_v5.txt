Prefill-decode disaggregation usually relies on RDMA or NVLink, which cheap single-GPU instances lack. For a TCP-linked fleet of such instances, we ask whether disaggregation is worth considering once chunked prefill, which attacks the same decode stalls, is tuned, and if so, where. Serving Gemma 4 26B and 31B with stock vLLM and NIXL/UCX on AWS, we measured both costs: a replica's longest inter-token gap followed the chunk size (26B, 4K inputs: 48 ms at 2,560 tokens, 21 ms at 512), and disaggregation's added time to first token (TTFT) was mostly KV transfer, which a larger UCX segment made 3.4x faster at low load. At 4K inputs and in chat, against tuned chunks, disaggregation served more per GPU under none of the latency targets our measured rates could rank; it won, by about 3x for 26B, only against replicas held at 2,560 tokens, near vLLM v0.30.0's floor for Gemma 4 with video enabled. A step-time model calibrated on our runs predicts that at low load and a 50 ms gap limit, inputs of roughly 15K-35K tokens (lower edge 10K-30K within its uncertainty) need so many chunks that disaggregation over TCP meets TTFT limits of a second or more that no chunk meets, given a small KV cache. Tune the chunk first.
