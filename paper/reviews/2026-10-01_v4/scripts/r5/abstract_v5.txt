Prefill-decode disaggregation usually relies on RDMA or NVLink, which cheap single-GPU instances lack. For a TCP-linked fleet of such instances, we ask whether disaggregation is worth considering once chunked prefill, which attacks the same decode stalls, is tuned, and if so, where. Serving Gemma 4 26B and 31B with stock vLLM and NIXL/UCX on AWS, we judged each request by its time to first token (TTFT) and longest inter-token gap. A replica's longest gap followed the chunk size (26B, 4K inputs: 48 ms at 2,560 tokens, 21 ms at 512), and most of disaggregation's added TTFT was the KV transfer, which a larger UCX TCP segment made 3.4x faster at low load. At 4K inputs and in chat, against tuned chunks, disaggregation served more per GPU under none of the latency targets our measured rates could rank. It won only against replicas held at 2,560 tokens, near vLLM's floor for Gemma 4 with video input (about 3x for 26B). A step-time model calibrated on our runs (out-of-sample TTFT within 8%) predicts that, with a small KV cache as from hybrid attention, disaggregation meets a band of TTFT limits (above 1.5 s at a 50 ms gap limit) that no chunk can beyond roughly 20K input tokens. Tune the chunk first.
